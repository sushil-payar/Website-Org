Hawaiian Legacy Reforestation Initiative is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 2014 to preserve economically viable, sustainable native Hawaiian forests — protecting endangered species, sequestering carbon, and recharging watersheds.

Generations ago, pineapple, sugarcane, and cattle replaced the endemic koa and ʻōhiʻa forests on Mauna Kea. In 2009, we planted a single koa tree and turned the corner on a century of degradation. Our forests span the Big Island of Hawaiʻi and Oʻahu.

We plant native Hawaiian trees on land stripped by a century of agriculture and cattle — rebuilding koa, ʻōhiʻa, iliahi, and other endemic species where they once thrived.

Protect

Our forests provide critical habitat for some of Hawaiʻi's rarest birds, protect the island's reef and ocean life, and recharge the watersheds that communities depend on.

ʻAha ʻUla Collection

Where conservation meets Hawaiian heritage.

In Hawaiʻi, feathered garments denoted status and were worn only by the most powerful aliʻi. Our reforestation re-established critical habitat for endemic species — some endangered — that make this living tradition possible again.

“This collection provides an important link between Hawaiian history and our efforts to preserve these rare forests for future generations.”
— Jeff Dunster, HLRI President
